Research

One question, seen from three sides.

How fast a signal changes, which way gravity points, how far a camera turned: these look like separate problems. I treat them as one. Each asks what a learned representation keeps, and whether that can be measured against a simple baseline that could have explained the result just as well.

I am drawn to the plain version of each question, a controlled world where the truth is known, a linear map small enough to inspect, a gate fixed in advance. When a result falls short, the shortfall is the finding, and I report how far short.

01

Structure by time scale

Can a representation be split into a fast-changing part and a slow-changing part, and the slow part split again?

02

Causes, not only trajectories

Does a video model encode the cause of a motion, or only the path that cause produces?

03

Geometry in frozen models

How much 3D structure survives in a model nobody retrained, and at what scale does it appear?

Research

V-JEPA causal geometry

A pre-registered pilot asking whether a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 video model represents the cause of motion (gravity) or only the trajectories that cause produces.

Pilot complete, main hypothesis not supported. The gravity direction is more alike across scenes than for raw pixels, but it cannot be told apart from a baseline that tracks the object’s trajectory.

Research

World-Models: Geometric Consistency Probe

Tests whether a frozen V-JEPA 2 video model represents 3D geometry, by fitting linear maps between its representations of rendered scenes before and after a known change.

Mixed. Most small-scale tests fall below their controls. The linear map beats “assume nothing changed” from 10° of camera rotation, and for yaw-only rotation it succeeds with about 1,000 training scenes.

About

A short introduction.

I work on the mathematical foundations of intelligence, with a current focus on causal representation learning. I am interested in recovering causal variables from observational data, organizing them into meaningful geometric structures within representation spaces, and learning efficient interactions between them.

I hold a B.S. from IIT Delhi, studied sociology at the Delhi School of Economics, and worked as an ML engineer before turning to AI research. Along the way I went deep into philosophy of science, especially the work of Thomas Kuhn and Noam Chomsky. I increasingly bring perspectives from philosophy and physics to theoretical questions about intelligence.

My focus is on studying the geometry of representation space: identifying increasingly abstract individual variables from input data, and finding the best geometry in which to represent them.

Influences

Thinkers behind the questions.

Philosophy, sociology, anthropology and the study of consciousness, read alongside the mathematics.

PHILOSOPHY OF SCIENCE

Thomas Kuhn

How scientific fields work inside a shared paradigm, and how they change when it breaks.

LANGUAGE AND MIND

Noam Chomsky

Structure and built-in constraints in language, a long-running reference point for debates on what learning systems need.

PHILOSOPHY AND SOCIOLOGY

Ludwig Wittgenstein

Meaning as use within shared practices. His ideas reached sociology through Peter Winch and ethnomethodology, and they recur in discussions of machine language understanding.

ANTHROPOLOGY AND CYBERNETICS

Gregory Bateson

Treated mind and evolution as processes of information and difference, in Steps to an Ecology of Mind.

SIMULATION AND VIRTUAL WORLDS

Jean Baudrillard

Sociologist and philosopher of simulation, whose Simulacra and Simulation is a standard text on constructed worlds and their relation to the real.

EVOLUTION OF CONSCIOUSNESS

Julian Jaynes

Proposed in The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind that consciousness is a recent, learned development of language and culture.
