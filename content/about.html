I work on the mathematical foundations of intelligence, with a current focus on causal representation learning. I am interested in recovering causal variables from observational data, organizing them into meaningful geometric structures within representation spaces, and learning efficient interactions between them.

I hold a B.S. from IIT Delhi, studied sociology at the Delhi School of Economics, and worked as an ML engineer before turning to AI research. Along the way I went deep into philosophy and the social sciences, and I increasingly bring perspectives from philosophy and physics to theoretical questions about intelligence.

My focus is on studying the geometry of representation space: identifying increasingly abstract individual variables from input data, and finding the best geometry in which to represent them.

Three thinkers shape how I frame the problem, and each converses with how world models and causal representation learning approach intelligence. Thomas Kuhn showed that scientists work inside a paradigm that shapes which questions and observations count; choosing a representation is a similar act, and changing it can change what becomes explainable. Jean Baudrillard (Simulacra and Simulation) asks how a constructed world relates to the real, which is the question a world model raises about its own simulation. Gregory Bateson (Steps to an Ecology of Mind) described information as a difference that makes a difference, close to what a causal variable is: a distinction that changes outcomes.

Read together, they point to my working view: intelligence appears as an emergent consequence of motion within an abstract space, which can be referred to as consciousness.
